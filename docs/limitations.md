# Limitations

Everything this project has **not** established, in one place.

The per-phase docs each carry a "what this does not establish" section. A
reviewer should not have to assemble them from four files, and a limitation
that is hard to find reads as a limitation someone hoped would not be found.

Read this alongside the results, not after them.

---

## The one that qualifies everything else

**Every number in this project comes from synthetic data.**

| Corpus | Source | Real? |
|---|---|---|
| `gst_in_synthetic` | Generated by `scripts/generate_synthetic_gst_invoices.py`, rendered by our own renderer | No |
| `fatura2` | Third party (Limam et al., CC-BY-4.0), 50 layout templates | Third-party, but still **template-generated** |

FATURA2 matters because it was designed by other people — it is what exposed
the extraction baseline scoring 1.000 on our invoices and 0.700 on theirs.

**SROIE is now converted: 652 photographs of real Malaysian retail receipts**
(MIT-licensed mirror), carrying real line-item annotations, which FATURA2 lacks
entirely. So the sentence above is no longer true of the whole project — but it
is still true of most of it.

| Corpus | Source | Real? |
|---|---|---|
| `gst_in_synthetic` | Our own generator and renderer | No |
| `fatura2` | Third party (Limam et al., CC-BY-4.0), 50 layout templates | Third-party, **but entirely synthetic** |
| `sroie` | ICDAR 2019, 652 photographed retail receipts | **Yes** |

What SROIE is **not** yet used for: Phase 1 (its annotations are line-level, so
box-IoU detection figures from it are not comparable with our word-level ones),
Phase 3, Phase 4 (no vendor history, no bank details, no GSTIN) and the VLM
run. Only the Phase 2 extraction baseline has met it.

**CORD remains unconverted.**

### What SROIE changed

| Corpus | Header accuracy | n |
|---|---|---|
| ours (our own renderer) | **1.000** | 50 |
| FATURA2 (third-party templates) | **0.700** | 50 |
| **SROIE (real photographs)** | **0.777** | 157 (test split) |

**Real photographed receipts score higher than FATURA2's synthetic templates.**
That is the opposite of the expected ordering, and it is not a measurement
artefact: all three are perfect-OCR ceiling numbers taken from gold text, so
image quality is not in play at all. What hurts a label-anchored baseline is
*layout diversity*, not degradation — FATURA2 deliberately varies 50 layouts
and sets the vendor name beside a logo, while receipts are structurally
monotonous (vendor at the top, `TOTAL` labelled). The difficulty of real
documents lives in the OCR stage, which these figures exclude by construction.

Two caveats specific to the SROIE figure:

- **Its macro field accuracy is inflated by null-vs-null.** SROIE annotates
  four field types; `gstin`, `phone`, `tax_id`, `due_date` and `po_number` are
  absent from its gold, and predicting nothing for them scores as correct.
  Header accuracy is the honest column.
- **Line-item F1 is 0.301**, and part of that was the converter rather than the
  extractor: receipts print the amount on the line *below* the description, so
  pairing by same-row overlap found only 37% of amounts until the rule was
  corrected to claim downward. It is 80% now.

### One missing label cost an entire field

The extractor returned null for `invoice_date` on **157 of 157** receipts. The
date was in plain text, in a format `_DATE_TOKEN` parses, directly beside a
label — but `_META_LABELS` knew only "invoice date" and "date of issue",
because it was written from Indian GST invoices. Receipts say "DATE".

A bare `date` anchor was added, ordered last so specific labels still win.
**It was fitted by inspecting the SROIE train split**, which is tuning and is
therefore declared:

| | header | critical-fields | macro |
|---|---|---|---|
| train, before | 0.687 | 0.005 | 0.701 |
| train, after | 0.771 | 0.176 | 0.725 |
| test, before | 0.684 | 0.019 | 0.699 |
| **test, after** | **0.777** | **0.217** | **0.726** |

Train and test move together, and FATURA2 independently improved 0.610 → 0.700
on the same one-line change while `gst_in_synthetic` stayed at 1.000. That
cross-corpus transfer is what separates a real fix from test-set tuning.

## The VLM has been run once, on one model

Qwen2-VL-2B, fp16 on a Colab T4, greedy, vision only, 100 documents. It **lost
to the regex baseline on both corpora** and did not close the generalisation
gap (−41 points versus the baseline's −39). That is a measurement, not a
limitation. What remains a limitation is everything around it:

- **One model, one configuration.** 2B is the smallest preset in
  `models/vlm.py`. `qwen2.5-vl-3b` (4-bit), `qwen2-vl-7b`, `internvl2-2b` and
  `florence-2-large` are all implemented and unrun, so "a VLM cannot close this
  gap" is **not** established — only that this one does not.
- **Hybrid mode is implemented and unmeasured.** `build_prompt(ocr_text=...)`
  puts the OCR transcript in the prompt, which refs [3] and [7] say should
  help. Every number here is vision-only.
- **The comparison is not apples to apples, and the asymmetry favours the
  baseline.** The rules figures use `--ocr-source gold`, a perfect-OCR ceiling.
  The VLM figures are genuine end to end. Re-running the baseline with
  `--ocr-source engine` is open work, and will lower it.
- **The prompt hard-codes `"currency": "INR"`.** Correct for our corpus, wrong
  for FATURA2, and it will have depressed currency-adjacent fields there. Left
  alone deliberately: changing it because the test set is known to be non-INR
  would be tuning against the test split.
- **6 of 100 responses did not parse** and are scored as fully missed
  documents. Some are probably truncation at the 1536-token budget rather than
  malformed output, so part of that 6% is a configuration artefact.
- **One run, no repeats.** Greedy decoding makes it reproducible, but there is
  no seed-variance or temperature sweep behind any of these numbers.
- **Only 2 of the 8 demo documents carry VLM output**, because the demo cache
  and the evaluation slice were selected independently. Screen 4 shows a real
  comparison on those two and "not run" on the rest.

## Phase 1 — preprocessing and OCR

- **Both exit criteria failed.** CER never reached < 8% on noisy pages (best
  `medium` is 0.0812 against a 0.0800 target; `heavy` is 0.4284), and
  preprocessing improves CER on `heavy` only, by 1.1 points. Reported as a
  result, not deferred.
- **The criteria were written against the wrong metric.** The full chain minus
  binarisation raises *word accuracy* 14.1 points on heavy pages while nudging
  CER the wrong way. Word accuracy is what Phase 2 depends on, because
  extraction needs to know *which field* a value belongs to, and that is
  spatial.
- **The first sweep, at n = 4, ranked the pipelines wrongly.** Re-running at
  n = 15 changed which pipeline wins in 4 of 6 metric/profile cells, and the
  headline deskew effect on `heavy` shrank from +21.6 to +11.0 points. Every
  qualitative conclusion survived; no magnitude did. Per-cell variance at n = 4
  exceeded the differences the ranking was built on, which is a caution about
  every thin cell in this project, not just that one.
- **Preprocessing is a net loss on clean pages** (word accuracy 0.5733 raw
  versus 0.4842 for the full chain) and the pipeline applies it
  unconditionally. Nothing decides whether a page is degraded enough to need
  it.
- **Detection metrics penalise tokenisation, not just recognition.** PaddleOCR
  splits punctuation into separate tokens (`Salai` `,`), so predicted word
  counts run ~1.8x gold and precision drops for reasons unrelated to reading
  quality.
- **The headline Phase 1 numbers are mobile-model numbers.** PP-OCRv6 medium
  cuts `medium` CER 7-fold (0.0812 → 0.0114) and **passes the CER exit
  criterion** there, at 2.8x the cost. It also re-orders the pipeline ranking
  in 3 of 4 measured cells. n=5 per cell.
- **The CER criterion fails on `heavy` under every configuration measured.**
  Best heavy CER is 0.2486 with v6-medium (0.4284 with mobile) against a 0.0800
  target, and preprocessing does not rescue it — `raw` is the best heavy row
  with both model families. So "the criterion is not met" is now a statement
  about the project, not only about `fast=True`.
- **The pipeline ranking is not a property of the pipeline.** It moved when n
  went 4 → 15 (4 of 6 cells) and again when the recognition model changed (3 of
  4 cells). Any "pipeline X is best" claim here is conditional on both.
- **The adaptive preprocessing policy now achieves best-of-both, after being
  rewritten.** It matches the best measured word accuracy in every profile
  (clean 0.5733, medium 0.4918, heavy 0.3062) and beats the better fixed
  configuration by 3.0 points on the mean.

  The first version gated each stage independently and bought only a tie. The
  cause was structural, not a mis-set threshold: `ABLATION_CONFIGS` is a
  cumulative ladder and the +14.1 points belongs to the whole chain, so an
  independently-composed subset could never reach it. The policy now decides
  only whether a page is degraded and applies `raw` or `full(no-binarize)`
  accordingly — both measured.

  Remaining costs, both real: triage runs on top of the chain it selects, so a
  degraded page pays ~1,342 ms against 618 for the fixed chain (about 3% of a
  page's OCR time); and it inherits `full(no-binarize)`'s **CER**, which is
  worse than `raw`'s on medium. Quote CER from the fixed configurations, not
  from `adaptive`.
- **Its calibration is against the mobile models**, and the picture changes
  with better recognition in a way that cuts against the policy: with v6-medium
  on `heavy`, preprocessing is worth **+22 points** of word accuracy (0.3077
  raw → 0.5304), twice the +11 the mobile models showed. A stronger recogniser
  benefits *more* from preprocessing on hard pages, not less.
- **Tesseract is implemented but never benchmarked.** It needs a system binary
  that is not installed here.

## Phase 2 — extraction

- **The ceiling-vs-end-to-end asymmetry has been measured, and it inverted the
  headline.** `rules` with real OCR scores **0.653** on our corpus against its
  1.000 ceiling, and 0.645 on FATURA2 against 0.700. So the VLM, which was
  reported as losing on both corpora, in fact **wins on our layouts by 19
  points** and loses on FATURA2 by 21. The 30-point generalisation gap that
  motivated the model argument was mostly the gap between perfect text and
  real text, not between home and foreign layouts.

  Every `rules` figure quoted elsewhere in these docs that is not explicitly
  marked end-to-end is still a ceiling.
- **Our degradation profiles are harsher than real paper, and that was
  invisible until SROIE.** Real OCR costs 34.7 points on our rendered invoices
  and 8.2 on photographs of real receipts. The `medium` and `heavy` transforms
  were tuned to look convincingly damaged and never checked against a
  photograph, so every Phase 1 figure is pessimistic about real scans by an
  unknown margin. The paired-corpus method is sound; its calibration is not
  evidence-based.
- **`invoice_date` collapses on real receipts under real OCR**, 0.580 → 0.032.
  The date is printed inside a line of register codes and OCR damage puts it
  past what glyph-folded fuzzy label matching recovers. It is the one field
  where photographs are decisively harder than renders.
- **FATURA2 has no line-item annotations.** Tag 10 is a single `table`
  placeholder covering the whole region, so every converted document carries
  `line_items: []`. The line-item exit criterion is verified **only on our own
  corpus**, where it is least meaningful.
- **FATURA2's content is Faker-generated nonsense, not just its layouts.**
  Inspected directly: item descriptions read "Prove amount sell." and "Assume
  against."; emails are `@example.com`; one document is dated `03-Jan-1994`
  with a due date of `06-Feb-2007`, thirteen years later; amounts are printed
  in `$` while the total is labelled `EUR`. The pages are pixel-perfect vector
  renders with no scan artefacts whatsoever.

  This matters for how its numbers are read. The **layouts** are genuinely
  third-party and that is what makes FATURA2 worth having — it is the only
  thing measuring whether a label-anchored baseline transfers to a design
  nobody here chose. But a share of the extraction failures on it are failures
  to extract meaning that is not there, and the corpus cannot distinguish the
  two. Treat FATURA2 as a **layout** benchmark, never as a document benchmark.
- **The FATURA2 tag vocabulary was inferred, not supplied.** The parquet ships
  bare integer `ner_tags` with no label names. The mapping was derived by
  auditing all 1,400 test documents; `--audit-tags` re-verifies it and fails if
  a release renumbers them. It remains an inference.
- **The rules baseline is tuned to a renderer we wrote.** Its rules are
  label-anchored rather than position-anchored to limit this, and FATURA2
  quantifies how much it still flattered: 1.000 at home, 0.559 away.
- **Single-page only.** The page-retrieval step from refs [3] and [4] is
  untouched.

## Phase 3 — RAG enrichment

- **The detectors catch what they were designed to catch, and nothing else.**
  Two scenarios were held out deliberately, and **both were missed completely**:

  | Held-out scenario | Caught |
  |---|---|
  | `line_item_padding` (line total inflated, invoice still reconciles) | **0 / 4** |
  | `gstin_state_contradicts_address` (state code vs. billing state) | **0 / 3** |

  Recall is 1.000 on designed-for scenarios and **0.800** once these are
  included. They are kept unfixed as a permanent probe: closing them and
  re-reporting 100% would reproduce the circularity they exist to expose.
- **Label space widened from 3 accounts / 10 descriptions to 6 / 21**, with
  deliberately confusable pairs: "Software Subscription" (a service, 5040)
  against "Laptop Docking Station" (hardware, 5020); "Annual Maintenance
  Contract - HVAC" (upkeep, 5050) against "Air Conditioner Unit" (the asset,
  5030); "Courier Charges" and "Freight Forwarding Service" (same account,
  almost no shared characters). **The macro-F1 of 0.996 quoted elsewhere was
  measured on the ten-description corpus and should be expected to fall.** The
  re-run is queued.

  Still far from real: a production chart of accounts runs to hundreds of
  codes, and 6 is not 300.
- **The generation step now exists, and the vote is its control.**
  `rag/generate.py` prompts a local Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct with the retrieved
  neighbours and their human-assigned categories, constrained to one account
  code or `ABSTAIN`. A run is queued; until its numbers land, every Phase 3
  figure quoted anywhere is a **weighted vote**, not a generated one, and is
  labelled as such.

  What the comparison is for: a generator that cannot beat a similarity-
  weighted vote over the *same* retrieved context has added a model and bought
  nothing. Reporting the generated path without the vote beside it would make
  "RAG works" unfalsifiable.

  Three guarantees the generator was built not to lose, each of which fails by
  producing a *better-looking* number rather than an error: it abstains (a
  generator that always answers shows higher coverage and is strictly worse);
  it is not prompted when retrieval found nothing (otherwise RAG has quietly
  become zero-shot classification); and only a code from the chart of accounts
  is accepted, so a disobedient answer becomes an abstention rather than a
  guess.

  Confidence comes from the retrieved evidence, not the model. A 0.5B model's
  self-reported certainty is worth nothing and log-probabilities over a 4-digit
  code are not calibrated.
- **Dense retrieval is implemented; its Phase 3 numbers are queued.**
  `rag/embed.py` is a MiniLM sentence encoder behind the same `Retriever`
  Protocol, verified against real weights (it retrieves "Wireless Mouse" for
  "cordless pointing device for a computer", which character n-grams cannot).
  Every Phase 3 figure quoted so far is **TF-IDF**, and `get_retriever` raises
  rather than falling back, so a report can never say "dense" over numbers
  TF-IDF produced.

  It may well not win. Character n-grams suit this task unusually well, because
  the variation retrieval has to survive is *surface* ("A4 PAPER 500SH"), which
  is what 3-5 grams capture and what a semantic embedding discards.
- **273 ledger entries.** Exact nearest-neighbour search is fine at this size
  and will need an approximate index at production scale.

## Phase 4 — vendor validation

- **Six fraud scenarios, all injected by us.** Two are held out and **both are
  missed completely**, so recall is 1.000 on the designed-for four and
  **0.524** including the probe:

  | Held-out scenario | Caught |
  |---|---|
  | `invoice_splitting` (a purchase broken into pieces under an approval ceiling) | **0 / 4** |
  | `quantity_inflation` (contract unit price, units never delivered) | **0 / 6** |

  Each is blind in a different way from the designed four. `invoice_splitting`
  is a pattern *across* invoices and every individual document in it is
  impeccable; the one check that looks across invoices keys on an identical
  total, which splitting avoids. `quantity_inflation` exploits the project
  validating what a vendor charges per unit but never **how many units it
  claims to have delivered**. Kept unfixed, like the Phase 3 probe.
- **70 synthetic suppliers, and legitimate bank changes now exist.** Four of
  them genuinely change bank account partway through their history. The system
  still flags those — correctly, on evidence this thin — but the
  false-positive rate is at last measured against a population containing
  honest changes rather than only fraud. On a probe corpus that moved precision
  from 1.000 to **0.750** and the false-positive rate on clean invoices from
  0.000 to **0.308**; the old figures were flattered by a corpus with no hard
  cases, and no threshold was adjusted to recover them.

  **Still absent:** name collisions between genuinely distinct entities,
  mergers, and subsidiaries sharing a GSTIN. All three are routine in a real
  supplier file and all three would produce findings this system would call
  fraud.
- **Vendor aliasing is implemented.** 24 of 70 suppliers also bill under a
  shortened trading name, and `VendorMaster.build` takes an explicit alias map
  the generator writes beside the corpus. Explicit, never inferred: fuzzy
  merging would also join `Arjun & Co` with `Arjun Pvt Ltd`, which this project
  deliberately keeps apart. Without the map a company billing under two names
  produces two records with half the history each, and a thin record is worse
  than none -- identity checks switch on at two invoices.

  What a real master has that this does not: aliases recorded by a human during
  onboarding, including ones that share no words with the legal name.
- **`address_mismatch` is now exercised.** Suppliers get a registered office
  plus 0-2 branches (44 of 70 in a probe corpus), minted per company rather
  than per invoice -- re-rolling per invoice would make the check fire on
  everything, which is the vendor-identity defect listed below. A known branch
  does not flag; an unseen address reaches REVIEW, never FAIL.
- **Duplicate detection is O(n) over history per invoice.** Fine at 100
  documents, needs an index in production.
- **An impostor vendor is indistinguishable from a legitimate new supplier.**
  On the invoice alone there is nothing to tell them apart — no record, no
  history, nothing to compare. The system raises `unknown_vendor` for both,
  correctly. Separating them requires an onboarding process outside this
  pipeline. `impostor_vendor` is therefore excluded from the scored fraud set;
  counting it would score 100% by construction.
- **Performance degrades with thin history, in a specific way.** At a
  50-invoice history: precision **0.778**, false-positive rate **0.214**. More
  vendors sit just above the two-invoice threshold where identity checks switch
  on but the record is still thin — enough history to be checked, not enough to
  be right.

## Phase 5 — service and human review

- **Token authentication with separated duties; not an identity provider.**
  The reviewer's name now comes from the credential, never from the request
  body — a body that disagrees is rejected rather than silently preferred, so
  a stale client fails loudly instead of writing a wrong name into the audit
  log. `SUBMIT` and `OVERRIDE` are distinct scopes, so the account that uploads
  an invoice cannot clear its own FAIL; without that separation the review
  queue is decoration.

  **What it is not.** Tokens are shared secrets compared in constant time and
  read from the environment. There is no password hashing, rotation, expiry,
  transport security or identity provider, so this authenticates a *caller*,
  not a *person*, and two people sharing a token are indistinguishable. Audit
  retention is still unbuilt. It is honest access control for a prototype on a
  trusted network and it is not a substitute for SSO.

  With no tokens configured the service runs exactly as before, every caller
  being `anonymous` with every scope — but it warns on startup and `/health`
  reports `auth: "disabled"`, because a control that is silently absent is
  worse than one that is absent.
- **The synchronous endpoint is not under 10 s with real OCR.** OCR is ~40 s a
  page on CPU. The exit criterion is met only by the job endpoint returning a
  handle immediately.
- **One correction does not teach the categoriser.** Measured: it took 3
  reviewed corrections of the same novel item before a later invoice was
  categorised without help, because Phase 3's vote lets several weak same-vendor
  neighbours outvote one near-exact match. The fix belongs in Phase 3.
- **With real OCR every page went to review** (6/6 on the timing run): OCR
  misreads of unit prices surface as price deviations and arithmetic flags. The
  auto-accept path is mostly exercised only with perfect text.
- **Accretion trusts auto-accepted documents.** A fraud that passes Phase 4 is
  added to the vendor master as normal history.
- **No load testing, one OCR worker, in-memory job registry** (jobs are lost on
  restart; the documents and decisions are not).
- **Single-page documents only.**
- **No reviewer study.** Whether the queue gives a person enough to decide well
  is untested.

## Statistical weight

| Experiment | n |
|---|---|
| Phase 1 ablation | 15 documents per cell |
| Phase 2 synthetic | 150 documents |
| Phase 2 FATURA2 | 300 documents |
| Phase 3 | 100 ledger / 50 evaluation |
| Phase 4 | 100 history / 50 evaluation |
| VLM run | 100 documents (50 ours, 50 FATURA2) |
| SROIE extraction | 157 documents (test split of 652) |

**A note on which numbers are current.** Several figures above were measured
before the corpus was made harder (6 accounts instead of 3, 21 item
descriptions instead of 10, branch addresses, trading names, legitimate bank
changes) and before dense retrieval and the generation step existed. Where that
is true it is said at the figure. The re-runs are queued; anything still
carrying an old number is marked, and nothing has been deleted in anticipation
of a better one.

Phase 1 is the thin one, and it is the one case where the thinness has been
measured rather than assumed: the first sweep ran at n = 4 and got the pipeline
ranking wrong in 4 of 6 cells. At n = 15 the 14-point word-accuracy effect on
`heavy` is well clear of the noise; differences under about 2 points between
adjacent pipeline rows are not, and should be read as ties rather than as an
ordering.

## The data has been wrong six times, and never loudly

Each was found only because a later phase depended on the data being right, and
each would have invalidated the phase that found it:

| Defect | Found in | Effect if unfixed |
|---|---|---|
| GSTIN check digits drawn at random (295/300 invalid) | Phase 2 | Checksum validation useless — fires on everything |
| `line_total` tax-inclusive; both checks double-subtracted discount | Phase 2 | 46/150 documents flagged; line-item arithmetic unusable as a signal |
| Vendor identity re-rolled per invoice (0/45 stable GSTIN or bank account); prices 57% spread; invoice numbers colliding | Phase 4 | Vendor master impossible; bank-change detection fires on every invoice |

Three more were in the **converters** rather than the generator, found while
adding SROIE:

| Defect | Found in | Effect if unfixed |
|---|---|---|
| `pages[0].dpi` emitted as null where the schema types it integer | Phase 2 | All 50 FATURA2 predictions fail schema validation, reading as the extractor emitting malformed documents |
| `annotator` / `currency` null, and `parties.buyer` null behind a `$ref` | SROIE | All 652 documents silently skipped at conversion, reading as "the mirror is unusable" |
| Line items paired by same-row overlap | SROIE | 37% of receipt amounts found; receipts print the amount on the line *below*, and the missing 63% went into line-item F1 as the extractor's fault |

**Not one of these six raised an error.** Every one produced a plausible
number. That is the pattern worth taking from this project: **a generator can
only be validated by something that consumes it**, and a converter only by
something that scores it. Phases 5 and 6 may yet surface a seventh.

## What would move the needle most

In order:

1. **Put SROIE through the stages that have not seen it.** It is converted
   (652 real receipts) but only the Phase 2 extraction baseline has run on it.
   The VLM has never seen a real photograph, and neither has Phase 1 — its
   line-level annotations make detection F1 incomparable, but CER and WER are
   perfectly valid there and unmeasured.
2. **Re-run the rules baseline with `--ocr-source engine`.** Until then the
   headline comparison puts a perfect-OCR ceiling beside a genuine end-to-end
   figure, which flatters the baseline.
3. **Try a bigger VLM and the hybrid image+OCR mode.** 2B vision-only lost; the
   3B/7B presets and the OCR hint are implemented and unrun, so the question
   the phase actually asked is still open.
4. ~~Run the VLM~~ — done, 100 documents, and it lost.
5. ~~Add a held-out fraud scenario to Phase 4~~ — done, two of them, both
   score 0.
